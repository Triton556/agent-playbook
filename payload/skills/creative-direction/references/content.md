# Content: angles and structure for the user's material

User material: see [task-brief-and-acceptance, 2. Understand the intent](../../dev-flow/references/task-brief-and-acceptance.md#2-understand-the-intent).

## Axes that make directions different

- **Angle:** what the piece is really about for the viewer (the same story can be about fatigue, about home, about learning to rest).
- **Promise:** why a stranger would keep watching or reading after ten seconds.
- **Structure:** chronological, question-driven, contrast between two moments, a single scene expanded, a list.
- **Voice and distance:** confession, observation, humor, explanation.
- **Format:** length, platform, series vs single piece.

## Direction as a moment

Write each direction as its opening: the first 15–30 seconds or first paragraph, with one sentence on its ending. Preservation: see [task-brief-and-acceptance, 2. Understand the intent](../../dev-flow/references/task-brief-and-acceptance.md#2-understand-the-intent).

## References

Find a few real pieces in the same format and audience (videos, essays, channels). Ask what the user likes in each: pacing, honesty, editing, thumbnails, titles. For channel or series decisions, connect the direction to the audience the user wants and to what the user can sustain.
