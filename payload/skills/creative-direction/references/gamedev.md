# Game design: from a liked experience to a game

A list of things the player enjoys (discovery, progress, rewards) is a description of motivation, not a game. The design starts from a place, a situation or a question that makes someone want to act, and from what the player actually does with their hands and head.

## Axes that make directions different

- **Core verb:** what the player mostly does - explore, build, decode, steer, combine, survive, talk.
- **Source of curiosity:** an unexplained place, a strange rule, a person's secret, a system to master.
- **Progression:** new abilities, new understanding, a changing world, unlocked spaces, relationships.
- **Fiction and setting:** where and when; chosen to make the verb and the curiosity feel natural.
- **Scale:** a single room or scene, a short episode, a full game.

## Direction as a moment

Write each direction as one moment of play: what the player sees, what they try, what happens, and why they want to continue. Add one line about the core loop that would repeat this moment.

## Respect scope

For a jam or a prologue, the direction must fit a playable slice: one place, a few verbs, one meaningful change. Keep the large vision separately and mark what belongs to the slice.

## Check cheaply

Before engine work, test the moment on paper or in a greybox: can a person understand the situation, form a guess and act on it? Note where they get stuck.

## Hand-over

The creative brief records the principle from the references the user named, the surface not to copy, the chosen moment and loop, the slice scope, and the open design questions.
