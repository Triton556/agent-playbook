# Visual, motion and 3D: one idea carried through

A collection of techniques is not a concept. A strong visual piece has one idea that every scene, model or frame serves; techniques are chosen because the idea needs them.

## Axes that make directions different

- **Core idea or metaphor:** what the piece is about in one sentence.
- **Structure in time or space:** one continuous transformation, a sequence of scenes with a through-line, an explorable space, a single still.
- **Visual language:** graphic or material, flat or deep, abstract or figurative, restrained or loud.
- **Rhythm:** music-driven, narrative-driven, interaction-driven.
- **Format and audience:** where it will be seen and what the viewer must understand in the first seconds.

## Direction as a moment

Describe each direction by its key frame or key moment and by how the next moment follows from it. Name the role of each technique (3D, typography, particles) in serving the idea; drop techniques that have no role.

## Sketch

Make a style frame or a short animatic per direction: a few frames with timing notes, or a still with the intended motion described. For 3D, a rough blockout from the target view. Watch motion at normal speed before choosing.

## Hand-over

Execution continues in visual-scenes or voxel-models. The creative brief records the core idea, the visual language, the rhythm source, the role of each technique and what must survive into the final piece.
