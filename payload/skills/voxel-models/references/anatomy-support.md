# Anatomical construction and planted support

Read for animals, creatures or people whose limbs must read in several views. These are original construction inferences from native grids, not recovered author recipes or tested walking rules.

Choose the pose before the primitive. A standing quadruped, seated dog, bird, gorilla and flying insect need different support grammars. Establish facing axis, chest/shoulder, pelvis/haunch, joint, ankle and foot landmarks. Derive proportions from those body landmarks; horns, wings, tails and held equipment can make overall bounds misleading.

Build each limb as overlapping tapered segments with deliberate joint offsets. Forelimbs and hindlimbs have different side profiles. Keep a local connection to the intended body root, then preserve air between the lower limbs. A bent silhouette requires geometry; a painted band on a straight rod is insufficient. Build a flat, directed foot/hoof/toe shape after the lower segment, with a readable ankle-to-sole transition.

Inspect front silhouette for left/right separation, side for joint direction, and top/ground plus a lower slab for front/rear placement. Use named foot regions to associate toes with a limb. In addition to [built-form’s connectivity rule](built-form.md#function-cavities-and-localized-wear), ground islands are not limb count: one inspected two-foot creature has ten claw islands at the ground but two foot masses higher up; a spider has six ground contacts and eight legs at the next slice. A staff can be an additional intentional contact.

For a chosen static standing pose, declare planted feet and their contact plane. Check the declared regions, root connections and gaps; inspect any unexpected support separately. A uniform-voxel centroid or footprint hull is only a balance heuristic. It does not prove dynamics, material mass distribution, a rig or a walk cycle.

For people, build body/limb chains first, then clothing layers, boots and gear sockets. Give asymmetry a cause: gesture, stance, tool or carried pack. Preserve a clear boot sole, toe, instep and shaft instead of extending the leg straight into the floor. Check gear attachment and hand clearance in the opposite view.

Evidence anchors: [kooow Creature Pack1](https://opengameart.org/content/voxel-creature-pack1), [55 creatures](https://opengameart.org/node/116478), [Astral_Journeyer dogs](https://journeyer.itch.io/voxel-dogs), and the built branch's humanoid/mech grids. Baseline beagle has 3×7-cell feet versus 3×4-cell ankle slices; this relationship is informative, not a universal dimension. Some legacy grids have black occupied interior cells; air is determined by format occupancy, not visible color.

In the study, detached 16×16 palette swatches were explicitly annotated and excluded from anatomical measurements only. Original files/hashes remain unchanged. Scene instances, export padding and palette swatches do not create new anatomy.
